## [TRAJDEBUG: Tracing Error Lifecycle to Identify Critical Failures in Long-Horizon Agent Trajectories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06346v1)
**Score:** 0.925  |  **arXiv:** 2608.06346v1

To address the challenge of pinpointing root-cause errors in long LLM agent trajectories, the authors introduce **TrajDebug**, a framework that traces error lifecycles using history compression and evidence-based analysis to locate and attribute critical failures. They also present **TrajErrBench**, a benchmark of 486 manually annotated failed trajectories across realistic coding and tool-use scenarios. Evaluated across diverse benchmarks, TrajDebug outperforms existing baselines and generates actionable diagnoses that improve downstream agent success rates.

## [Chained Recursive Language Models for Multi-Iteration Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05124v1)
**Score:** 0.920  |  **arXiv:** 2608.05124v1

To prevent error propagation during complex long-context reasoning, this paper introduces Chained Recursive Language Models (Chained RLM), an inference-time architecture that breaks tasks into a sequence of fresh model calls. Instead of maintaining a bloated conversational history, each step receives the original problem alongside compact plain-text summaries, a shared blackboard, and task artifacts from previous runs, allowing the model to incrementally inspect and correct prior work. The authors detail the system's handoff architecture and evaluate when this fresh-context approach outperforms both direct LLM prompting and standard recursive tool-calling.

## [ABSeeker: Training Long-Horizon Search Agents via Answer-Backtracked Credit Assignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05102v1)
**Score:** 0.916  |  **arXiv:** 2608.05102v1

This paper introduces Answer-Backtracked Credit Assignment (ABC), a framework for training long-horizon search agents that traces backward from ground-truth answers to evaluate and reward individual search steps rather than relying on coarse, trajectory-level feedback. By applying ABC to both supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, the authors train **ABSeeker**, a 4B-parameter model, using just 8.5k examples. On complex web-browsing benchmarks (BrowseComp and BrowseComp-ZH), ABSeeker significantly outperforms same-scale search agents and matches the performance of models nearly eight times its size (~30B).

## [Beyond Top-K: Replacing Black-Box Retrieval with Interpretable Agentic Operations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06305v1)
**Score:** 0.915  |  **arXiv:** 2608.06305v1

Standard embedding-based retrieval often fails on complex financial and regulatory documents because chunking severs crucial relationships between numbers, headers, and units. To solve this, the authors introduce READ, an interpretable, embedding-free agent that navigates raw documents using deterministic lexical search and structural operations to create a replayable audit trail. Evaluated on a 780-page government financial report, READ significantly outperforms dense retrieval (58.8% accuracy vs. 15.7%), demonstrating that structured, lexical navigation is vastly superior to vector similarity for tabular and context-dependent text.

## [Reward Structure Shapes the Interaction Between Episodic Exploration and Neural Memory in Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05111v1)
**Score:** 0.911  |  **arXiv:** 2608.05111v1

This paper demonstrates that episodic exploration bonuses and neural memory architectures act as complements in partially observable reinforcement learning: bonuses drive state exposure, while memory converts that exposure into performance. Crucially, the authors show that this interaction depends on the task's underlying reward structure—what the reward actually supervises—rather than mere reward density. To organize these dynamics, they introduce a theoretical framework using observation-anchored reward machines that categorizes tasks by their memory retention burdens and predicts when exploration bonuses will enhance memory architectures.

## [Argus: A General-Purpose Agentic Runtime for Long-Horizon Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05144v1)
**Score:** 0.910  |  **arXiv:** 2608.05144v1

**Argus** is a multi-agent runtime designed for complex, long-horizon tasks that enables LLMs to self-correct and evolve without retraining model weights by maintaining persistent project state, specialized role interactions, and strict verification gates. By accumulating verified strategies and learning from rejected paths, the framework dynamically pivots upon failure and becomes increasingly token-efficient over time. Evaluation across diverse domains shows significant performance gains, including a ~78% success rate on SWE-Bench Pro (up from 59% for a direct copilot) alongside real-world successes in GPU kernel optimization, mathematical data synthesis, and automated paper writing.

## [Toward Skill-Native LLMs: Skill Entropy for Benchmarking and Training Long-Horizon Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05139v1)
**Score:** 0.910  |  **arXiv:** 2608.05139v1

This paper introduces "Skill Entropy" to quantify the difficulty LLMs face when switching between distinct reasoning skills during complex, multi-step tasks, along with a 558-skill benchmark ($\text{Skill}^2\text{-Bench}$) to evaluate this capability. Experiments reveal that state-of-the-art models suffer significant accuracy drops on high-entropy tasks, prompting the authors to develop Skill-Entropy RL—a training framework that rewards models for predicting both correct answers and their corresponding skill sequences. This RL approach drastically improves long-horizon reasoning performance (e.g., boosting Qwen3-4B accuracy from 34.4% to 68.4%) and serves as a reusable training signal for standard open-source datasets.

## [RRC: Unlocking Generative Reward Models in LLM Reinforcement Learning via Ranking-Based Reward Construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06310v1)
**Score:** 0.903  |  **arXiv:** 2608.06310v1

Generative reward models excel at relative response ranking, but struggle in LLM reinforcement learning because standard RL algorithms require absolute scalar scores. To bridge this gap, the authors propose Ranking-based Reward Construction (RRC), a method that converts comparative preference rankings into effective scalar RL signals using self-competitive sampling and anchor-guided reference responses. Experiments across open-ended chat and reasoning benchmarks show that RRC significantly improves RL training efficiency and performance compared to existing reward conversion approaches.

## [AV-AIVAT: 74x Cheaper Agent Evaluation with Certified Anytime-Valid Stopping in Imperfect-Information Games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06362v1)
**Score:** 0.896  |  **arXiv:** 2608.06362v1
**Length:** 34 pages

Evaluating game-playing agents is expensive because proving a true skill difference through raw outcomes requires thousands of hands to filter out luck. To solve this, the authors introduce **AV-AIVAT**, which combines variance-reduction techniques with continuously monitored confidence sequences to enable safe, statistically valid early stopping the moment a result is conclusive. Tested on poker benchmarks with LLM agents, the method reduces the required number of game hands by up to 74x while providing exact, third-party auditable statistical guarantees.

**Why this matches:** This paper aligns with your interest in LLM evaluation and AI builder tooling, sharing the overlapping keywords "agents" and "evaluation." It presents a practical approach to agent evaluation by introducing a method that makes evaluating agents in imperfect-information games 74x cheaper.
