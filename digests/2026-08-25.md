## [Prime Agent: A Self-Improving RLM Harness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23552v1)
**Score:** 0.934  |  **arXiv:** 2608.23552v1
**Length:** 16 pages

**Prime Agent** is an open-source harness designed for long-horizon coding and autonomous agent workflows that leverages a persistent IPython environment, cross-trajectory memory, and recursive subagents to maximize test-time compute and context management. By standardizing execution, recovery, and verification, it enables models to reliably construct their own problem-solving strategies without being bottlenecked by framework failures. Across diverse evaluations, the system substantially boosts model performance—raising ARC-AGI-3 RHAE Best@1 from 30% to 95.5% while matching or outperforming existing harnesses on complex tasks like GPU-kernel generation, emulator building, and open-ended gameplay.

## [ReWorld: An Interactive World Model with Long-Horizon Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23565v1)
**Score:** 0.907  |  **arXiv:** 2608.23565v1
**Length:** 21 pages

**ReWorld** is an interactive, real-time world model that resolves the tension between responsive short-term control and unbounded long-term memory. It achieves this by decoupling local and global attention during training, utilizing a pose-indexed landmark retrieval cache under a fixed memory budget at inference, and applying LoRA-based distillation for 4-step real-time streaming. Across diverse simulated and real-world environments, it achieves state-of-the-art action fidelity and visual quality while reliably recalling scenes during minute-long rollouts where traditional sliding-window models fail.

## [The Interaction Tax: When Communication Erases Diversity in Multi-Agent Teams](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23541v1)
**Score:** 0.899  |  **arXiv:** 2608.23541v1
**Length:** 14 pages

This paper investigates why multi-agent LLM collaboration often fails to beat simple independent sampling, identifying an "interaction tax" where sharing full solutions causes diverse models to prematurely converge on the first idea they see. Evaluating 11 optimization tasks under matched compute budgets, the authors show that independent generation better preserves diversity, demonstrating that multi-agent performance depends on exchanging targeted information at the right time rather than sharing complete outputs.

## [AUSO: Action-Level Unified Skill Optimization from Internalization to Utilization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21292v1)
**Score:** 0.897  |  **arXiv:** 2608.21292v1

**AUSO** is a progressive reinforcement learning framework designed to manage the full lifecycle of an agent's skills, transitioning them from initial external supervision into selective, autonomous decision-making. Rather than applying uniform skill guidance across entire trajectories, it evaluates individual actions in both skill-conditioned and skill-free contexts to amplify beneficial skill use and suppress distractions. Evaluated on ALFWorld, WebShop, and SearchQA, the approach consistently outperforms competitive baselines in both overall task performance and out-of-distribution generalization.

## [SWE Refactor Bench: Can Coding Agents Complete a Long-Horizon, Whole-Repository Stack Migration?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23564v1)
**Score:** 0.874  |  **arXiv:** 2608.23564v1

To address benchmark loopholes where agents copy legacy code to pass tests without actually refactoring, the authors introduce **SWE Refactor Bench**, a benchmark of 20 whole-repository migrations evaluated on both migration completeness and behavioral correctness. Testing eight frontier models across 520 runs revealed that current agents struggle significantly with long-horizon migrations, achieving only a 5.4% overall pass rate and leaving 13 of the 20 tasks completely unsolved. Performance dropped sharply on complex tasks—scoring 31.4% on build toolchain updates but just 5.6% on language rewrites—establishing a rigorous testbed for researchers working on autonomous repository-level software engineering.

## [Prompt-Model Interaction Reaches the Fixed Points: A deterministic, task-free structural readout -- and the factorizations of it that failed](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21315v1)
**Score:** 0.873  |  **arXiv:** 2608.21315v1
**Length:** 11 pages

This paper investigates whether prompt sensitivity is an artifact of specific benchmark tasks or an intrinsic feature of a model's conditional distribution by using a deterministic, task-free readout: the fixed-point structure of short-window token generation. The authors find that conditioning on as few as nine prefix tokens dramatically reshapes this structural property and reorders models far more than extensive instruction tuning does. Crucially, multiple intuitive explanations—such as prefix length, markup formatting, and attention-sink mechanics—fail to predict these shifts, demonstrating that prompt-model interactions are fundamental and difficult to factor into simple, universal rules.

## [Asymmetric Capacity Allocation in Self-Refinement Pipelines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21345v1)
**Score:** 0.873  |  **arXiv:** 2608.21345v1

This paper presents the first systematic study on how model size impacts each stage—generation, critique, and revision—within LLM self-refinement pipelines. The authors find that generation and revision require larger models to avoid degrading performance, whereas the critique stage needs only a small model to be effective and is largely insensitive to scaling. Consequently, researchers can design more compute-efficient multi-stage systems by allocating larger model capacities to generators and refiners while using lightweight models for critics.

## [How to Train a Critic Stably and Efficiently](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23566v1)
**Score:** 0.868  |  **arXiv:** 2608.23566v1

To overcome the instability of critic-based reinforcement learning in LLMs—which often forces reliance on expensive multi-sample methods like GRPO—the authors introduce **Best-Practice Critic Optimization (BPCO)**. BPCO stabilizes training by combining bounded value predictions, length-adaptive advantage estimation, and privileged training signals like reference answers or grading rubrics. Across mathematical reasoning benchmarks on models up to 30B parameters, BPCO matches or outperforms group-based baselines while requiring only a single sampled response per prompt.

## [CLEAR: Continuous Latent Adapter Routing for Utility-Preserving LLM Safety Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21278v1)
**Score:** 0.868  |  **arXiv:** 2608.21278v1

To mitigate the utility loss caused by global safety tuning, the authors introduce **CLEAR** (Continuous Latent Adapter Routing), a framework that uses a lightweight hidden-state gate to dynamically adjust the activation strength of a safety LoRA adapter over a frozen model backbone. By applying safety adaptations conditionally rather than globally, CLEAR protects benign prompts from unnecessary performance degradation while still blocking harmful inputs. In evaluations on Llama-3-8B-Instruct, CLEAR reduced HarmBench attack success rates from 32.3% to 0.5% while achieving up to 7.1 percentage points higher GSM8K accuracy than standard SFT or LoRA methods.
