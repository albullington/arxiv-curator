## [AUSO: Action-Level Unified Skill Optimization from Internalization to Utilization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21292v1)
**Score:** 0.897  |  **arXiv:** 2608.21292v1

**AUSO** is a progressive reinforcement learning framework designed to manage the full lifecycle of an agent's skills, transitioning them from initial external supervision into selective, autonomous decision-making. Rather than applying uniform skill guidance across entire trajectories, it evaluates individual actions in both skill-conditioned and skill-free contexts to amplify beneficial skill use and suppress distractions. Evaluated on ALFWorld, WebShop, and SearchQA, the approach consistently outperforms competitive baselines in both overall task performance and out-of-distribution generalization.

## [Prompt-Model Interaction Reaches the Fixed Points: A deterministic, task-free structural readout -- and the factorizations of it that failed](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21315v1)
**Score:** 0.873  |  **arXiv:** 2608.21315v1
**Length:** 11 pages

This paper investigates whether prompt sensitivity is an artifact of specific benchmark tasks or an intrinsic feature of a model's conditional distribution by using a deterministic, task-free readout: the fixed-point structure of short-window token generation. The authors find that conditioning on as few as nine prefix tokens dramatically reshapes this structural property and reorders models far more than extensive instruction tuning does. Crucially, multiple intuitive explanations—such as prefix length, markup formatting, and attention-sink mechanics—fail to predict these shifts, demonstrating that prompt-model interactions are fundamental and difficult to factor into simple, universal rules.

## [Asymmetric Capacity Allocation in Self-Refinement Pipelines](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21345v1)
**Score:** 0.873  |  **arXiv:** 2608.21345v1

This paper presents the first systematic study on how model size impacts each stage—generation, critique, and revision—within LLM self-refinement pipelines. The authors find that generation and revision require larger models to avoid degrading performance, whereas the critique stage needs only a small model to be effective and is largely insensitive to scaling. Consequently, researchers can design more compute-efficient multi-stage systems by allocating larger model capacities to generators and refiners while using lightweight models for critics.

## [CLEAR: Continuous Latent Adapter Routing for Utility-Preserving LLM Safety Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21278v1)
**Score:** 0.868  |  **arXiv:** 2608.21278v1

To mitigate the utility loss caused by global safety tuning, the authors introduce **CLEAR** (Continuous Latent Adapter Routing), a framework that uses a lightweight hidden-state gate to dynamically adjust the activation strength of a safety LoRA adapter over a frozen model backbone. By applying safety adaptations conditionally rather than globally, CLEAR protects benign prompts from unnecessary performance degradation while still blocking harmful inputs. In evaluations on Llama-3-8B-Instruct, CLEAR reduced HarmBench attack success rates from 32.3% to 0.5% while achieving up to 7.1 percentage points higher GSM8K accuracy than standard SFT or LoRA methods.

## [Rethinking Expressivity and Efficiency in Test-Time Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21308v1)
**Score:** 0.867  |  **arXiv:** 2608.21308v1

The authors introduce $\text{E}^2$-TTT, a method that resolves the trade-off between expressive per-token updates and hardware-efficient chunk-wise processing in Test-Time Training. By utilizing a closed-form state transition that exactly reproduces per-token fast-weight and momentum states, it enables fully parallelized chunk-level training while preserving critical temporal update dynamics. Evaluated on models up to 1.3B parameters, $\text{E}^2$-TTT matches the throughput and language modeling performance of existing methods while significantly improving in-context retrieval and long-context extrapolation (retaining over 90% accuracy at $8\times$ the training context length).

**Score:** 0.852  |  **arXiv:** 2608.21356v1

This paper introduces the "Salt method," a framework that uses automated formal verification kernels to validate AI-generated proofs and prevent hallucinations in end-to-end engineering pipelines. To demonstrate this, a single researcher directed a fleet of AI agents over five weeks to take high-level application code through a verified compiler and tape out a RISC-V processor with zero human-written RTL or manual proof review. The full accounting provides a blueprint for using rigorous machine verification as a scalable, cost-effective referee for autonomous software and hardware design.

## [SPARCL: Spectral Partitioned Analytic Continual Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21307v1)
**Score:** 0.844  |  **arXiv:** 2608.21307v1

This paper identifies "spectral interference"—where new tasks distort the shared feature autocorrelation spectrum—as the primary cause of forgetting in closed-form, analytic continual learning. To resolve this, the authors introduce SPARCL, a method that partitions the autocorrelation matrix into core and residual subspaces, freezing old-class components along dominant spectral directions while updating only the residual. Across several vision benchmarks with frozen ViT backbones, SPARCL provides theoretical invariance guarantees for old-class predictions and substantially closes the performance gap to top continual learning baselines without storing exemplars.
