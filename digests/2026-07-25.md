## [Notes to Self: Can LLMs Benefit from Experiential Abstractions?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20372v1)
**Score:** 0.923  |  **arXiv:** 2607.20372v1

This paper investigates whether Large Language Models (LLMs) can improve their problem-solving by distilling past reasoning attempts into a retrievable library of reusable, natural-language strategies. By testing these abstractions during inference-time retrieval and reinforcement learning, the authors demonstrate consistent performance gains across mathematical and logical reasoning benchmarks. Notably, self-extracted abstractions perform as well as teacher-derived ones and transfer across different models and datasets, showing that LLMs can independently generate and apply their own experiential knowledge.

## [Train the Model, Not the Reader: Decodability Supervision for Verifiable Activation Explanations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20379v1)
**Score:** 0.875  |  **arXiv:** 2607.20379v1

**Summary for Researchers:**

Current methods for explaining AI activations rely on reconstruction metrics that only capture general "gist," making them blind to individual false claims and deceptive "private codes." To fix this, the authors introduce RECAP, a training technique that uses auxiliary linear heads to ensure specific internal features remain independently checkable without sacrificing model performance. In experiments, RECAP eliminates unfaithful encoding tricks and allows simple probes to reliably flag adversarial lies in generated explanations (AUC 0.95 vs. 0.51 for standard models).

## [PyroDash: Cost-Efficient Token-Level Small-Large Language Model Collaborative Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20327v1)
**Score:** 0.870  |  **arXiv:** 2607.20327v1
**Length:** 19 pages

**PyroDash** is a cost-aware inference framework where a small language model (SLM) dynamically hands off generation to a frozen large language model (LLM) at the token level by emitting a learned control token. By internalizing the offloading policy directly into the SLM through reinforcement learning, the system eliminates the need for a separate routing model, LLM retraining, or access to LLM logits. On mathematical reasoning benchmarks, PyroDash delivers flexible trade-offs, outperforming an LLM-only baseline by 6.36 percentage points in accuracy while reducing overall inference costs by 20.4%.

**Why this matches:** This paper introduces PyroDash, a new architectural approach for cost-efficient, token-level collaborative inference between small and large language models, aligning with your interest in novel system architectures and practical tooling for building AI systems. ## [Beyond Sycophancy: Structured Resistance and Compliance in LLM Moral Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21558v1)
**Score:** 0.864  |  **arXiv:** 2607.21558v1

Instead of treating sycophancy as a simple failure mode, this paper investigates how LLMs revise moral judgments under social influence using principles from human social psychology. Across three empirical studies, the authors show that a model's willingness to change its mind depends on the distance of the incoming opinion, who is presented as holding it, and group consensus. This work provides a framework to help researchers differentiate between principled, constructive belief revision and unprincipled sycophancy in language models.

## [SoftReason: A Fully Differentiable Neuro-Soft-Symbolic Deductive Reasoning Architecture over High-Dimensional Perceptual Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.20402v1)
**Score:** 0.854  |  **arXiv:** 2607.20402v1

**SoftReason** is a fully differentiable neuro-soft-symbolic architecture designed to perform logical deductive reasoning directly over high-dimensional perceptual inputs (like images) and knowledge graphs. By replacing discrete symbolic interfaces with soft interpretation tensors and a learned, differentiable immediate-consequence operator, it eliminates gradient gaps and allows end-to-end training across perception and multi-step logic. The authors validate the framework on Knowledge-aware Visual Question Answering (KVQA), demonstrating its ability to jointly optimize visual grounding, knowledge injection, and deductive reasoning.
