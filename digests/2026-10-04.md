## [Reconstruct, Practice, Go Real: Guided Self-Improvement for Embodied Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02204v1)

**RPG (Reconstruct, Practice, Go Real)** is a framework that autonomously improves robot manipulation capabilities without updating model weights by practicing in simulation to iteratively refine symbolic skills and multimodal LLM prompts. Across 22 simulated tasks, the approach dramatically increased success rates from 28.6% to 95.0%, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines. Crucially, the resulting frozen system transferred reliably to a physical robot, achieving a 100% success rate across 30 real-world trials.

## [From Gradients to Capabilities: Understanding Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02179v1)
**Score:** 0.865  |  **arXiv:** 2610.02179v1

This paper investigates the underlying optimization mechanics of multi-teacher on-policy distillation, analyzing how disparate teacher signals translate into student parameter updates and task performance. The authors reveal that Adam’s momentum significantly homogenizes diverse teacher gradients, BF16 rounding masks nearly 90% of subtle master-weight updates, and the choice between token- and response-level loss averaging heavily skews response weighting. Crucially, they demonstrate that while top-64 vocabulary KL approximation reliably mimics full-gradient updates, downstream capabilities like math accuracy hinge critically on these underlying loss-averaging strategies.

## [Decoding Looped Transformers Better for (Almost) Free](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02185v1)
**Score:** 0.861  |  **arXiv:** 2610.02185v1
**Length:** 32 pages

This paper introduces **LoopCD**, a training-free contrastive decoding framework for looped Transformers that improves generation quality by contrasting final predictions against naturally occurring, "weaker" intermediate states from earlier recurrent loops. By leveraging these internal representations without requiring auxiliary models, the method delivers substantial performance gains across reasoning and coding benchmarks. Crucially, these improvements allow models to halve their recurrent iterations while still matching or exceeding full-depth baselines, reducing inference compute by 22.5% to 48.2%.

## [The Missing Primitive: Diagnosing and Repairing Mathematical Reasoning in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02191v1)
**Score:** 0.860  |  **arXiv:** 2610.02191v1
**Length:** 27 pages

This paper evaluates whether LLMs possess true structural mathematical understanding by introducing "Mathematical Primitives" and a benchmark that tests four distinct reasoning dimensions: discovery, generation, digestion, and execution. The diagnosis reveals that a failure in "discovery"—identifying the core mathematical concept or primitive—is the dominant bottleneck limiting model performance, rather than the ability to execute the solution steps. To address this, the authors propose a primitive-guided self-distillation training framework that successfully repairs these discovery gaps and boosts mathematical reasoning across challenging benchmarks.

## [TACO: Ternary Absolute-max Column-wise One-sparse Optimizer for LLM Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02199v1)
**Score:** 0.847  |  **arXiv:** 2610.02199v1
**Length:** 24 pages

**TACO** is a highly memory-efficient optimizer designed for full-parameter LLM fine-tuning that avoids the performance degradation seen in previous memory-reduction methods. By computing steepest-descent updates using only the sign of the largest-magnitude gradient element per matrix column, it slashes persistent optimizer state memory by up to $174\times$ compared to 8-bit AdamW while matching its accuracy and speed. Crucially, this efficiency enables full-parameter fine-tuning of 30–32B parameter models on a single 80 GB GPU.

## [Trust the Direction, Search the Step: Zero-and-First-Order Methods for LLM Fine-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02190v1)
**Score:** 0.834  |  **arXiv:** 2610.02190v1

The authors propose Zero-and-First-Order (ZFO) optimization, a lightweight framework for LLM fine-tuning that decouples the update direction from the step-size selection. By taking the direction from a standard first-order optimizer and using just two extra forward passes to evaluate local curvature along that line, ZFO performs an efficient, adaptive line search at a fraction of the usual cost. Supported by theoretical convergence guarantees, the method consistently improves optimization stability and downstream performance over fixed-step baselines across multiple models and benchmarks.

## [Hierarchical Continuous Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02193v1)
**Score:** 0.832  |  **arXiv:** 2610.02193v1

This paper introduces Hierarchical Continuous Diffusion Language Models (HC-DLM) to overcome key flaws in diffusion LMs: discrete models sever token dependencies during parallel decoding, while continuous models lack intermediate grounding in valid token configurations. HC-DLM resolves this by maintaining a persistent continuous latent trajectory that reads out discrete tokens at each step, using them as structured feedback to guide the next latent update. At matched model sizes, the framework outperforms both discrete and continuous diffusion baselines across structured reasoning (Sudoku), planning (Countdown), and standard language modeling (LM1B).
