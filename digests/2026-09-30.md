## [KV-streams for Efficient Compaction in Agentic Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35750v1)
**Score:** 0.939  |  **arXiv:** 2609.35750v1

This paper introduces **KV-streams**, a plug-and-play method that eliminates the training throughput bottlenecks of context compaction in agentic LLMs by streaming the KV cache forward rather than repeatedly flushing and re-prefilling it. Compatible with multiple compaction techniques, it achieves a 2.6x to 5x wall-clock training speedup with no loss in performance. Crucially, the authors show that standard reinforcement learning alone enables this streamed cache to naturally function as a recurrent memory, retaining information long after it leaves the active context window.

## [Harness Learning Enables Generalizable Test-Time Adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35738v1)
**Score:** 0.931  |  **arXiv:** 2609.35738v1

This paper introduces "harness learning," a meta-learning framework where a reinforcement-learning-trained model iteratively rewrites an LLM agent's orchestration code (tool use, control flow, and prompting) based on execution feedback instead of modifying model weights. Evaluated on reasoning and multi-hop question-answering benchmarks, this parameter-free method successfully generalizes to unseen tasks at test time and improves performance across multi-round revisions. It is well worth reading for researchers interested in test-time adaptation, agent architecture search, and continual learning without gradient updates.

## [Shockingly Simple Self-retrospection Improves Agentic Models Without RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35741v1)
**Score:** 0.897  |  **arXiv:** 2609.35741v1
**Length:** 62 pages

This paper introduces Retrospection-Only Fine-Tuning (ROFT), an approach where language model agents improve by fine-tuning via standard next-token prediction exclusively on their own self-generated explanations of past attempts, bypassing reinforcement learning and external teachers. On SWE-bench benchmarks, ROFT matches or outperforms GRPO with greater sample efficiency and no verifier, even learning to solve tasks where all initial attempts had failed. These results demonstrate that learning to explain past experiences effectively transfers to better future actions, offering a simpler, lightweight alternative to reward-based policy updates.

## [New LoRA Skills Should Read but Never Write](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31600v1)
**Score:** 0.895  |  **arXiv:** 2609.31600v1

To resolve the interference that occurs when merging multiple LoRA adapters, this paper introduces **READ** (Read-only Expansion of Adapter Deltas), a sequential composition method that avoids both retraining and dynamic routing. The approach converts adapters into a canonical form and restricts coupling to a single direction, allowing newly added skills to read information from prior skills without altering or overwriting their outputs. The resulting composed update folds directly into the base model with zero added inference cost and substantially outperforms existing adapter-merging baselines across multiple benchmarks.
