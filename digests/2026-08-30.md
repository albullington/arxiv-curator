## [RATIO: A Benchmark for Retrieval Across Typed Ideation Operations in Scientific Literature](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27394v1)
**Score:** 0.926  |  **arXiv:** 2608.27394v1

This paper introduces **RATIO**, a large-scale benchmark designed to evaluate how effectively retrieval models find scientific papers to support three distinct ideation goals: directly solving a problem (*Address*), generalizing it (*Broaden*), or finding concrete implementations (*Specify*). Constructed from millions of computer science papers using distant supervision alongside LLM and human vetting, RATIO provides both training and evaluation frameworks for literature-grounded discovery systems. The authors show that while fine-tuning models on these specific ideation operations boosts retrieval performance, current systems still leave substantial room for improvement.

Across various target models and execution harnesses, it consistently outperforms existing baseline attackers in jailbreak success rate, tool efficiency, and transferability.

## [Consolidating RLVR Capabilities Across Domains: A Deep Dive into Fusion Paradigms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27409v1)
**Score:** 0.885  |  **arXiv:** 2608.27409v1

This paper systematically compares three paradigms for combining domain-specific RLVR expert models into a single LLM: weight merging (Merge), joint-data training (Mix RL), and multi-teacher distillation (MOPD). Ultimately, the study provides a practical decision framework to help researchers choose the best fusion strategy based on their compute budget, whether pre-trained experts already exist, and specific capability goals.

## [Boosting LLM Exploration via Weak-Model Guidance in RLVR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27420v1)
**Score:** 0.873  |  **arXiv:** 2608.27420v1
**Length:** 13 pages

To prevent the loss of reasoning diversity (entropy collapse) common in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), this paper proposes forcing the target LLM to complete partial reasoning paths generated by a smaller, weaker model. This simple perturbation disrupts the model's overconfidence and broadens its exploration space without requiring extra supervised fine-tuning, prompt engineering, or complex reward designs. Across mathematical benchmarks, the method consistently outperforms standard RLVR, yielding especially strong performance gains on pass@$k$ metrics as $k$ scales up.

## [SWE-Prime: Fewer Trajectories, Better Performance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27449v1)
**Score:** 0.870  |  **arXiv:** 2608.27449v1
**Length:** 9 pages

This paper introduces **SWE-Prime**, a data selection framework that addresses the noise, redundancy, and risky steps often present in successful software-engineering agent trajectories. It uses a two-stage filtering process to select high-quality trajectories and compute training loss only on the most valuable step segments while maintaining the full sequence context. By fine-tuning on just the top 10% of curated data, the method outperforms training on the entire dataset, improving performance on SWE-Bench Verified by up to 24.2%.

This paper introduces **Persona-Execution Separation (PES)**, an architectural pattern that divides an LLM agent into two distinct trust domains: a flexible "persona" (instructions, tone, prompts) and an audited, faceless "execution" layer. By connecting these domains through governed contract bridges and data-loss prevention rules, PES allows organizations to frequently update and iterate on agent personalities without compromising state traceability or requiring costly execution re-validation. Validated on a regulated enterprise platform, this work offers actionable design principles for researchers and engineers deploying multi-user LLM agents under strict audit and compliance requirements.
