## [Recursive Experiential-Working Memory Evolution for Long-Horizon Agent Harnesses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24876v1)
**Score:** 0.944  |  **arXiv:** 2608.24876v1

This paper introduces **Recuris**, a dual-memory architecture designed to overcome history bloat and inaccurate skill selection in long-horizon LLM agent tasks. By using working memory to ground current task needs and a meta-agent to recursively update experiential skill memory based on execution failures, the system enables bounded recursive self-improvement. Across four benchmarks and ten models, Recuris significantly boosted task success rates (by up to +32.2 points on the longest tasks) while cutting common long-horizon failure modes by up to 80%.

## [Prime Agent: A Self-Improving RLM Harness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23552v1)
**Score:** 0.934  |  **arXiv:** 2608.23552v1
**Length:** 16 pages

**Prime Agent** is an open-source harness designed for long-horizon coding and autonomous agent workflows that leverages a persistent IPython environment, cross-trajectory memory, and recursive subagents to maximize test-time compute and context management. By standardizing execution, recovery, and verification, it enables models to reliably construct their own problem-solving strategies without being bottlenecked by framework failures. Across diverse evaluations, the system substantially boosts model performance—raising ARC-AGI-3 RHAE Best@1 from 30% to 95.5% while matching or outperforming existing harnesses on complex tasks like GPU-kernel generation, emulator building, and open-ended gameplay.

## [Structurally-bounded Agentic Graph Exploration for Evidence-Grounded Scholarly DeepSearch](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24809v1)
**Score:** 0.933  |  **arXiv:** 2608.24809v1

By using this bounded and transparent graph-based process, it achieves up to 3× higher recall@50 than proprietary agent baselines on benchmark datasets while reducing computational costs by roughly two-thirds.

## [ReWorld: An Interactive World Model with Long-Horizon Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23565v1)
**Score:** 0.907  |  **arXiv:** 2608.23565v1
**Length:** 21 pages

**ReWorld** is an interactive, real-time world model that resolves the tension between responsive short-term control and unbounded long-term memory. It achieves this by decoupling local and global attention during training, utilizing a pose-indexed landmark retrieval cache under a fixed memory budget at inference, and applying LoRA-based distillation for 4-step real-time streaming. Across diverse simulated and real-world environments, it achieves state-of-the-art action fidelity and visual quality while reliably recalling scenes during minute-long rollouts where traditional sliding-window models fail.

## [The Interaction Tax: When Communication Erases Diversity in Multi-Agent Teams](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23541v1)
**Score:** 0.899  |  **arXiv:** 2608.23541v1
**Length:** 14 pages

This paper investigates why multi-agent LLM collaboration often fails to beat simple independent sampling, identifying an "interaction tax" where sharing full solutions causes diverse models to prematurely converge on the first idea they see. Evaluating 11 optimization tasks under matched compute budgets, the authors show that independent generation better preserves diversity, demonstrating that multi-agent performance depends on exchanging targeted information at the right time rather than sharing complete outputs.

## [AUSO: Action-Level Unified Skill Optimization from Internalization to Utilization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21292v1)
**Score:** 0.897  |  **arXiv:** 2608.21292v1

**AUSO** is a progressive reinforcement learning framework designed to manage the full lifecycle of an agent's skills, transitioning them from initial external supervision into selective, autonomous decision-making. Rather than applying uniform skill guidance across entire trajectories, it evaluates individual actions in both skill-conditioned and skill-free contexts to amplify beneficial skill use and suppress distractions. Evaluated on ALFWorld, WebShop, and SearchQA, the approach consistently outperforms competitive baselines in both overall task performance and out-of-distribution generalization.

## [StarHarness: Evolving Harnesses with Stratified Search for Enterprise Environments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24804v1)
**Score:** 0.897  |  **arXiv:** 2608.24804v1

**StarHarness** is an optimization framework that evolves an LLM agent's execution environment—including prompts, tool interfaces, and subagent structures—to fix model-environment mismatches without modifying underlying model weights. Guided by a stratified search based on baseline failure patterns, it improves agent performance by 20–35 percentage points across complex IT, ITSM, and finance benchmarks. Notably, these harness improvements generalize to held-out tasks and transfer seamlessly across different model families, including GPT and Qwen.

## [Reading Is Not Using: Retrieval, Judgment, and the Design of AI Financial Research Workflows](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24842v1)
**Score:** 0.888  |  **arXiv:** 2608.24842v1

This paper identifies a "retrieval-integration gap" in LLM-based financial analysis, demonstrating that while models can accurately find key risk disclosures in long contexts, they often fail to incorporate that retrieved information into their final investment judgments. The authors find that this failure worsens as context length increases across multiple model families, exposing a major flaw in evaluating AI analysts based solely on retrieval accuracy. Finally, they show that this issue can be resolved by redesigning workflow architectures to place targeted, structured restatements of key information immediately adjacent to the decision-making step.
