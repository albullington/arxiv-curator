## [On the Fragility of Self-Improving Agents: Variance, Task Order, and Underspecification](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18066v1)
**Score:** 0.940  |  **arXiv:** 2608.18066v1

This paper investigates the reliability of memory-based self-improving agents, revealing that their reported gains are highly fragile due to high evaluation variance and a heavy reliance on favorable task orderings (implicit curricula). The authors trace this instability partly to task and environment underspecification, demonstrating that even adding detailed feedback and rubrics only partially mitigates performance drops. Consequently, the work advocates for more rigorous, multi-run evaluation protocols and better human oversight mechanisms to properly benchmark autonomous agents.

## [Rollplex: Cross-Phase GPU Spatial Sharing for Vision Language Model Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14498v1)
**Score:** 0.924  |  **arXiv:** 2608.14498v1
**Length:** 16 pages

**Rollplex** is a post-training runtime for vision-language models (VLMs) that eliminates GPU underutilization caused by strictly sequential on-policy reinforcement learning phases. It achieves this by overlapping visual and prompt prefix computations with rollout decoding, using phase-aware memory management and layout-adaptive weight sharing to avoid memory overflow across different tensor-parallelism configurations. On 32 H800 GPUs, it preserves synchronous RL semantics while delivering up to a 1.30× speedup over serial execution and up to 2.24× over disaggregated baselines.

## [Delegation Asymmetry in Agentic Recommender Systems: Measuring Two-Sided Receptivity in Online Dating](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18058v1)
**Score:** 0.915  |  **arXiv:** 2608.18058v1

Using large-scale survey data from a major dating platform ($N > 5,500$), this paper investigates user willingness to both deploy and receive autonomous conversational LLM agents. The authors identify a stark "delegation asymmetry"—users are roughly three times more likely to send an AI agent on their own behalf than to engage with someone else's agent, resulting in successful interactions in only 4–13% of random pairings. To address this bottleneck, the paper models platform design interventions, demonstrating that routing agent communications based on receiver receptivity can triple per-contact engagement.

## [Don't Drop the BATON: Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation via Agentic Subtask Exploration and Transition-aware Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16889v1)
**Score:** 0.894  |  **arXiv:** 2608.16889v1

This paper introduces BATON, a training-free framework that scales long-horizon robot manipulation by exploring and caching modular subtasks independently rather than searching across entire multi-stage tasks. To prevent compounding errors, it equips an LLM-coordinated vision-language-action (VLA) pipeline with transition-aware memory mechanisms that verify, align, and smoothly connect the entry and exit conditions between sequential subtasks. By reducing test-time exploration costs from exponential to additive ($T \times K$), BATON achieves an 11.6% task success improvement over state-of-the-art methods on the RoboMemArena benchmark.

## [You Only Pass Once: Answering and Abstaining Together in a Single Forward Pass of a Frozen Language Model](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14465v1)
**Score:** 0.893  |  **arXiv:** 2608.14465v1
**Length:** 24 pages

This paper introduces **YOPO (You Only Pass Once)**, a method that resolves the activation interference that occurs when simultaneously applying activation steering (to improve reasoning) and probe-based abstention (to avoid confabulation when information is insufficient) in a frozen LLM. By using a lightweight network to reconstruct clean residual states from steered ones, YOPO accomplishes steering, answering, and abstaining in a single forward pass without doubling inference compute. Evaluated across multiple model families and question-answering benchmarks, YOPO outperforms both frozen and two-pass baselines while preserving robust zero-shot cross-domain abstention.

**Why this matches:** This paper aligns with your interest in practical tooling and novel architectural approaches for AI systems by introducing a method for frozen language models to answer and abstain together in a single forward pass.

## [Proteus: Incremental Memory Activation for Long-Context Sequence Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16844v1)
**Score:** 0.890  |  **arXiv:** 2608.16844v1

Existing long-context memory models often suffer from "memory pollution" because static capacity allows early tokens to consume excessive representational space, hurting the retention of later context. To fix this, the authors propose **Proteus**, a zero-cost, plug-and-play mechanism that progressively expands effective memory capacity as the sequence grows, forcing tighter early compression and preserving room for incoming data. Across multiple state-of-the-art architectures (such as Titans and Hope-Attention), Proteus consistently improves performance on language modeling, reasoning, and long-context retrieval, with benefits scaling at longer context lengths.

## [Chain-of-Experience for Continual LLM Improvement](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18027v1)
**Score:** 0.885  |  **arXiv:** 2608.18027v1

This paper introduces **Chain-of-Experience (CoE)**, a test-time approach that enables large language models (LLMs) to iteratively improve their performance through interaction loops using self-feedback and environmental signals (like execution results). Across math, coding, and knowledge benchmarks on eight frontier LLMs, CoE achieves an average 5.6% accuracy gain and a 19% reduction in API costs compared to feedback-free baselines, offering higher token efficiency than conventional test-time compute methods. The authors also demonstrate that stronger base models benefit the most from CoE and that the framework remains robust even when faced with noisy or weak feedback.

**Score:** 0.883  |  **arXiv:** 2608.18008v1

This paper formalizes hybrid LLM-planner and RL-controller architectures by framing LLM progress feedback as potential functions within a Goal-Augmented MDP. The authors theoretically prove that this approach guarantees policy invariance—meaning the true optimal policy is preserved even if the LLM's evaluation scores are inaccurate or noisy. Numerical experiments validate this theoretical guarantee on a small MDP, confirming that optimal behavior is retained even under heavily scaled, adversarial feedback.
