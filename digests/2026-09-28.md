## [DeepEdu-v1: Efficient and Scalable Agentic LLMs for Vietnamese Education](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31568v1)
**Score:** 0.906  |  **arXiv:** 2609.31568v1

DeepEdu-v1 is an on-premise AI tutoring system designed for Vietnamese education that addresses data-sovereignty constraints, curriculum-specific hallucinations, and GPU memory bottlenecks. It introduces a cluster-level long-context inference engine to cut prefill latency, paired with a self-improving agentic layer that builds a verified local knowledge base over time instead of requiring model fine-tuning. In deployed benchmarks, the system achieves nearly a 2x time-to-first-token speedup over standard vLLM while boosting agentic task accuracy from 70.0% to 79.5%.

## [New LoRA Skills Should Read but Never Write](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31600v1)
**Score:** 0.895  |  **arXiv:** 2609.31600v1

To resolve the interference that occurs when merging multiple LoRA adapters, this paper introduces **READ** (Read-only Expansion of Adapter Deltas), a sequential composition method that avoids both retraining and dynamic routing. The approach converts adapters into a canonical form and restricts coupling to a single direction, allowing newly added skills to read information from prior skills without altering or overwriting their outputs. The resulting composed update folds directly into the base model with zero added inference cost and substantially outperforms existing adapter-merging baselines across multiple benchmarks.

## [Multi-agent Scaling Across Disjunctive and Compensatory Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31563v1)
**Score:** 0.889  |  **arXiv:** 2609.31563v1
**Length:** 25 pages

Using Steiner’s task taxonomy, this paper evaluates how multi-agent LLM teams scale across disjunctive tasks (where one correct answer suffices) and compensatory tasks (where outputs are averaged, like Fermi estimation). The authors find that scaling team size offers surprisingly little practical benefit: plurality voting fails to capture the latent gains of larger teams, multi-round discussion saturates with just a single peer, and averaging estimates is bottlenecked by shared model biases. Ultimately, the study demonstrates that simply adding more agents is ineffective unless the aggregation mechanism is carefully aligned with the underlying task structure.

## [Trust Guided Decision Transformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31586v1)
**Score:** 0.878  |  **arXiv:** 2609.31586v1

Decision Transformers often degrade during long rollouts because their context drifts out of distribution, which this paper shows can be directly detected through elevated next-state prediction errors. To address this, the authors propose Trust Guided Decision Transformer (TGDT), which uses a calibrated error threshold to filter out unreliable context windows before using a critic to select the highest-value action from the trusted candidates. Evaluated on D4RL benchmarks, TGDT reduces compounding rollout errors and outperforms standard Decision Transformers as well as reset- and value-only context selection baselines.

## [Strategically Diverse Sampling for Self-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31571v1)
**Score:** 0.872  |  **arXiv:** 2609.31571v1

This paper proposes that self-training is far more effective when data is sampled for "strategic diversity"—meaning substantively different problem-solving approaches—rather than standard IID responses filtered purely for correctness. Using two sampling techniques (including GROOT, a new hierarchical tree-based method), the authors show that strategically diverse self-training substantially improves performance on difficult reasoning tasks, RL initialization, and test-time scaling. Remarkably, training on diverse but *incorrect* reasoning traces from a 4B model outperformed standard distillation from a 235B teacher, suggesting that approach diversity can matter more than correctness or model scale.

## [User Model Extraction via Belief Self-Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31603v1)
**Score:** 0.871  |  **arXiv:** 2609.31603v1

This paper introduces Belief Self-Distillation (BSD), an unsupervised framework that allows researchers to both decode and causally manipulate the internal representations large language models form about their users. The authors demonstrate that altering these inferred user models can directly trigger or bypass safety refusals without changing the user's actual prompt. Additionally, they find that independently trained LLMs naturally converge on a shared geometric structure for user representations, making this paper relevant for researchers working on interpretability, steering, and AI safety.

## [EAServe: Encode-Aware Disaggregated Serving for Multimodal Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31551v1)
**Score:** 0.865  |  **arXiv:** 2609.31551v1
**Length:** 13 pages

Serving multimodal LLMs introduces a media "Encode" stage that existing disaggregated serving frameworks fail to balance, resulting in underutilized encode GPUs and bottlenecked downstream prefill and decode stages. To solve this, **EAServe** treats the Encode stage as a central control point, using load-adaptive micro-batching, rate-controlled prefill offloading, and automated Bayesian resource tuning to balance the three-stage pipeline. Across image, video, and audio models, it improves GPU utilization and achieves up to 1.7× and 4.3× higher SLO-constrained goodput compared to vLLM and NVIDIA Dynamo, respectively.

## [Learning to Stop without Learning to Stop: Self-Supervised Confidence Training Improves Reasoning Efficiency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31619v1)
**Score:** 0.843  |  **arXiv:** 2609.31619v1

This paper demonstrates that training reasoning models to predict their own intermediate confidence significantly improves efficiency without using explicit length penalties or inference-time early-stopping mechanisms. Using a self-supervised objective on just 600 problems, the approach cuts reasoning tokens by up to 25% across multiple models and benchmarks while matching original accuracy. The findings suggest that reasoning efficiency naturally emerges from learning metacognitive signals, offering a promising alternative for researchers focused on reducing LLM inference costs.

## [Online Learning via Learned Latent Bayesian Tracking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31559v1)
**Score:** 0.828  |  **arXiv:** 2609.31559v1

While Bayesian filtering offers a principled approach to online learning under non-stationary conditions, applying it to high-dimensional deep networks is computationally prohibitive. To solve this, the authors propose AURA, a meta-learning framework that learns a low-dimensional latent space for parameter dynamics, enabling efficient online adaptation via Extended Kalman Filtering before reconstructing the full model. Evaluated on dynamic wireless receivers and non-stationary image classification, AURA delivers single-step online updates with significantly higher speed, accuracy, and efficiency than existing baselines.
