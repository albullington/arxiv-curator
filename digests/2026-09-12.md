## [ConvMem: Convolutional Memory for Long-Context Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10441v1)
**Score:** 0.911  |  **arXiv:** 2609.10441v1

**ConvMem** is a training-free, highly parallelizable framework that tackles the high latency and error accumulation of sequential memory methods in long-context LLM reasoning. By treating the prompted LLM as a convolutional kernel, it hierarchically processes text segments across a logarithmic tree rather than a linear chain, utilizing skip connections and multi-kernel query decomposition to capture evidence robustly. On multi-hop QA benchmarks, ConvMem outperforms existing training-free baselines while avoiding the out-of-distribution overfitting typical of reinforcement-learning-based approaches.

## [Artificial Id: Drive and Persistent Alignment in Agentic AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11911v1)
**Score:** 0.906  |  **arXiv:** 2609.11911v1

To move beyond rigid, hand-crafted control rules in continuous AI agents, this paper proposes an "artificial id"—an internal drive mechanism that lets agents autonomously determine when to persist, adapt, or halt behavior without explicit task-level objectives. Using a minimal virtual testbed, the authors demonstrate that while this drive enables adaptive behavior to emerge organically, it can also cause unintended strategies and misalignments to stubbornly persist across tasks. Ultimately, the work argues that AI alignment must shift from evaluating single-trajectory outputs to establishing persistent governance boundaries over an agent's ongoing state, identity, and authority.

## [JarvisGUI: Towards Cross-Device GUI Agents with Dynamic Task Composition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10451v1)
**Score:** 0.886  |  **arXiv:** 2609.10451v1

To address the limitations of single-device evaluations, this paper introduces **JarvisGUI**, a benchmark that dynamically composes and tests agent workflows across heterogeneous operating systems, including Android, Windows, and Ubuntu. By modeling tasks as typed input-output transformations, the framework evaluates how effectively agents handle multi-step coordination, cross-platform context, and intermediate state transfers. Experiments show that current state-of-the-art open-source agents struggle significantly on these cross-device tasks, exposing a critical capability gap overlooked by existing static benchmarks.

## [MindTopo: Can Foundation Models Reason in Topological Space?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11900v1)
**Score:** 0.883  |  **arXiv:** 2609.11900v1

The authors introduce **MindTopo**, a benchmark designed to test whether multimodal foundation models understand deformation-invariant spatial relations—such as knots, enclosure, and continuity—across both passive reasoning and interactive planning tasks. Evaluating 14 multimodal models and several video generators reveals that all models perform substantially below human baselines, with a particularly steep drop in performance on planning tasks compared to static reasoning. The findings demonstrate that current models fail to reliably preserve underlying topological dynamics across transitions, exposing a critical blind spot in spatial AI.

## [Forgetting Only What Matters: Layer-Selective Unlearning toward Robust LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10439v1)
**Score:** 0.880  |  **arXiv:** 2609.10439v1
**Length:** 22 pages

The authors introduce FOM-UL, a layer-selective unlearning framework that identifies and updates only the transformer layers most critical to target data, thereby preserving general model utility. By concentrating updates instead of altering the entire network, this approach prevents forgotten knowledge from resurfacing after 4-bit and 8-bit post-training quantization. Across multiple standard benchmarks, FOM-UL consistently outperforms leading unlearning baselines in erasure effectiveness, utility retention, and resistance to adversarial extraction.

## [CoRA-NAS: Coarse Ranking and Anchor-Residual Refinement for Neural Architecture Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11884v1)
**Score:** 0.880  |  **arXiv:** 2609.11884v1

To address the unreliability of zero-cost proxies across diverse search spaces, **CoRA-NAS** introduces a two-stage framework that pairs an initialization-based coarse ranking prior with an anchor-based learning-curve refinement. At roughly 1% of the compute cost of full training—and without requiring fully trained architecture labels—it extrapolates early validation trajectories from a few anchor models to correct candidate rankings. Across four major NAS benchmarks, it delivers high ranking consistency (Spearman correlations of 0.715–0.946), offering the highest worst-space robustness among compared methods while identifying near-optimal architectures.

## [On the Regularization Landscape for the Linear Recommendation Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11876v1)
**Score:** 0.878  |  **arXiv:** 2609.11876v1

This paper unifies leading deep-learning-inspired linear recommendation models under a single theoretical framework, revealing that they fundamentally rely on either nuclear-norm or Frobenius-norm regularization. The authors show that nuclear-norm models provide closed-form, low-rank solutions with limited expressiveness, whereas Frobenius-norm models are more expressive but computationally heavier or full-rank. Addressing this trade-off, the paper introduces two novel regularizers that combine the strengths of both approaches by delivering closed-form, low-rank, and highly expressive solutions.

## [Show-Harness: Just a VLM Agent Can Play Robots](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10522v1)
**Score:** 0.876  |  **arXiv:** 2609.10522v1

**Show-Harness** introduces a semantic action interface that enables standard vision-language models (VLMs) to directly control robots by mapping high-level semantic reasoning to local, embodiment-specific physical actions. The framework supports zero-shot control with closed-source frontier models, lightweight fine-tuning for small open-source VLMs, and includes a GUI tool (GUMI) to collect demonstrations without specialized teleoperation hardware. By outperforming existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) baselines in cross-task and cross-embodiment generalization, the paper shows that the right interface can unlock robot capabilities from foundation models without expensive, embodiment-specific pretraining.

## [IBIB: A Protocol for Measuring Enterprise AI Systems by Serving Route, Not Model Identifier](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10494v1)
**Score:** 0.867  |  **arXiv:** 2609.10494v1
**Length:** 42 pages

This paper introduces IB2, an evaluation protocol that benchmarks enterprise AI systems by their complete serving infrastructure—including routing, precision, and execution harnesses—rather than nominal model checkpoint names. Testing across 11 systems, the authors demonstrate that identical model weights vary significantly in performance and reliability depending on how they are served, and that standard benchmarks distort rankings by omitting execution failures. It is a worthwhile read for researchers focused on production LLM reliability, system-level benchmarking, and the gap between raw model capabilities and deployed enterprise performance.
