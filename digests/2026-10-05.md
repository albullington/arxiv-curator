## [MRVQ: One Resident Index for Dimension- and Rate-Elastic Vector Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03651v1)
**Score:** 0.883  |  **arXiv:** 2610.03651v1

This paper introduces Matryoshka Residual Vector Quantization (MRVQ), a method that allows a single stored index to dynamically adapt to varying bit-rates and embedding dimensions simply by truncating its codes. By serving all operating points from one artifact, MRVQ cuts index RAM by roughly 18–22× compared to maintaining separate per-rate indices and outperforms standard baselines like PQ and OPQ. While it trades off some retrieval accuracy compared to specialized, separately tuned models, it offers a practical, low-memory solution for production retrieval systems needing elastic latency and memory budgets.

## [What Should World Models Forget? Stratified Retention for Continual Adaptation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03713v1)
**Score:** 0.874  |  **arXiv:** 2610.03713v1

Unlike standard continual learning settings where all forgetting is treated as a failure, world models in dynamic environments must discard outdated instance-level facts while permanently preserving foundational invariants like physics. Because existing metrics cannot distinguish valid knowledge revision from catastrophic forgetting, they erroneously favor frozen models. To address this, the authors introduce a framework of *stratified retention* based on invariance timescales, alongside an evaluation method (*differential retention*) that jointly tracks invariant stability and factual revision latency.

## [LESSER: Post-Training Data Selection with Output-Layer Gradients](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03702v1)
**Score:** 0.873  |  **arXiv:** 2610.03702v1

Gradient-based data selection effectively curates LLM post-training data, but computing full-parameter backward passes across large candidate pools is computationally prohibitive. To solve this, the authors introduce LESSER, which demonstrates that computing gradients for only the output layer—requiring just a cheaper forward pass—is sufficient for effective selection. This drop-in approach cuts feature-extraction FLOP costs by up to 9.7× for supervised fine-tuning and 3.0× for reinforcement learning while matching the downstream performance of full-gradient methods.

## [Pivot-SD: Efficient Self-Distillation for Masked Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03665v1)
**Score:** 0.849  |  **arXiv:** 2610.03665v1

**Pivot-SD** is an efficient offline self-distillation framework for masked diffusion language models that addresses token-level credit assignment by isolating high-impact decision points ("pivots") based on uncertainty reduction. Rather than training on entire sequences or steps, it selectively reinforces successful pivots with cross-entropy and penalizes failure-inducing pivots via targeted unlikelihood. Using just 200 questions, the method enables LLaDA-8B-Instruct to outperform both full-sequence supervised fine-tuning and budget-matched diffusion RL baselines on math and coding benchmarks.

## [When May a Bandit Leave Its Anchor? E-Process-Authorized Thompson Sampling under Non-stationarity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03646v1)
**Score:** 0.847  |  **arXiv:** 2610.03646v1
**Length:** 25 pages

The authors introduce e-process-authorized Thompson sampling (e-ATS), a bandit algorithm that uses an anytime-valid e-process to statistically govern when an arm is permitted to discard past history and adapt to non-stationarity. Under stationary conditions, it provides a finite-sample guarantee that the probability of ever deviating from standard optimistic Thompson sampling is bounded by a chosen false-alarm level $\alpha_E$ without tuning heuristic thresholds. Empirical evaluations show this evidence-based gate effectively prevents premature adaptation, making it an appealing read for researchers interested in safe, principled changepoint detection in multi-armed bandits.

## [Planning to Learn](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03667v1)
**Score:** 0.838  |  **arXiv:** 2610.03667v1

By framing classification through reinforcement learning, this paper shows that policy gradients underperform cross-entropy because they are myopic, optimizing only immediate gains rather than accounting for remaining training time. The authors propose the "horizon loss," a one-line modification that smoothly interpolates from cross-entropy toward the exact policy gradient as training progresses. On benchmarks including ImageNet with ResNets and Vision Transformers, this objective improves top-1 accuracy over cross-entropy, offering especially large gains in the presence of label noise.
