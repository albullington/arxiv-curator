## [AVA-Encoder: Towards Agent-Native Video Representation Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12313v1)
**Score:** 0.925  |  **arXiv:** 2608.12313v1

This paper introduces **AVA-Encoder**, a framework that transforms videos into structured Knowledge Graphs linked with multimodal assets, enabling AI agents to easily query, edit, and reason over cinematic content. The system uses a textual-gradient auto-encoding loop that reconstructs videos from the knowledge graph, using reconstruction errors to automatically optimize both the representation and the encoding policy. Experiments show it significantly outperforms existing baselines and human-tuned policies, and the authors release their full framework, a video reconstruction benchmark, and a new dataset of film knowledge graphs.

## [VAKRA: Evaluating Multi-Hop Reasoning Across APIs and Retrieval Under Tool-Use Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12282v1)
**Score:** 0.919  |  **arXiv:** 2608.12282v1

**VAKRA** is a new benchmark featuring over 8,000 executable APIs designed to evaluate AI agents on multi-hop reasoning, document retrieval, and natural-language policy compliance. Evaluations of top frontier and open-weight models reveal sharp performance drops on complex tasks, with accuracy falling to ~50% on multi-hop workflows and as low as 2.4% on policy-constrained unanswerable queries. Trace analysis demonstrates that these failures are primarily caused by higher-level language reasoning issues—such as entity disambiguation and cross-source grounding—rather than basic tool execution mechanics.

## [Convergent Detour Hijacking: Task-Preserving Resource Amplification in Skill-Based LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12273v1)
**Score:** 0.915  |  **arXiv:** 2608.12273v1

This paper introduces Convergent Detour Hijacking (CDH), a text-only attack that tricks LLM agents into taking costly, unnecessary tool "detours" while still successfully completing the original task. Across 491 evaluation tasks, the attack increased token consumption by ~67% and execution time by ~92% on tested models without significantly dropping overall task completion rates. The findings highlight a key security flaw in modular LLM agents, proving that successful task outcomes do not guarantee execution integrity or cost safety.

## [One Frozen Simulator Is Not Enough: Simulator Collapse in Multi-Agent RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12253v1)
**Score:** 0.910  |  **arXiv:** 2608.12253v1
**Length:** 41 pages

Training multi-agent RL policies against a single LLM user simulator leads to "simulator collapse," where the policy overfits to narrow LLM behaviors and generalizes poorly to real humans. To solve this, the authors introduce *Verbalized Sampling* to broaden simulator responses during inference, alongside *Co-Training* to optimize policies against a dynamic population of trainable simulators. Evaluated across three multi-turn benchmarks and a human study, these methods boost policy success with unseen users by up to 14%, supported by the release of their open-source SCOPE framework.

**Why this matches:** This paper addresses simulator collapse in multi-agent reinforcement learning, aligning with your interest in multi-agent architectures and evaluating practical environments for agent systems.

## [AI4AI at Test-Time: Strong-to-Weak Capability Transfer via Harnesses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12307v1)
**Score:** 0.900  |  **arXiv:** 2608.12307v1
**Length:** 23 pages

This paper explores "strong-to-weak scaffolding," a method where a stronger AI model automatically designs and refines test-time code harnesses to improve a weaker model's task performance without modifying its parameters. Across four Theory-of-Mind benchmarks, these automated harnesses nearly doubled weaker target-model accuracy (from 0.49 to 0.91) by offloading unstable reasoning steps into deterministic code, routing, and strict formatting. The findings demonstrate that inference-time harness engineering is a highly effective, retraining-free complement to traditional model distillation.

The system uses a multimodal LLM to iteratively refine input text prompts for semantic accuracy and artifact reduction, while employing Bayesian optimization to automatically tune generation seeds and guidance scales. In human evaluation studies, this approach significantly improved video quality and text adherence over standard baselines, achieving preference win rates up to 69%.

## [DreamFly: Causal Memory and Receding-Horizon Diffusion Planning for Aerial Vision-Language Navigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12308v1)
**Score:** 0.880  |  **arXiv:** 2608.12308v1
**Length:** 24 pages

**DreamFly** is a diffusion-based aerial vision-language navigation (VLN) framework that addresses key VLA model limitations regarding context memory, short planning horizons, and goal termination. It introduces a causally aligned historical memory to prevent future data leakage, a receding-horizon ($K$-step plan, execute-one) diffusion planner for robust closed-loop control, and a lightweight explicit stop-estimation module. Evaluated on the OpenFly benchmark, DreamFly achieves state-of-the-art success rates and the lowest navigation errors across both seen and unseen environments.

## [Diagram-MMU: A Multi-Modal Benchmark for Scientific Diagrams](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12262v1)
**Score:** 0.874  |  **arXiv:** 2608.12262v1

Diagram-MMU is a new multimodal benchmark featuring 3.7k scientific diagrams and 18.3k human-validated questions designed to evaluate MLLMs on diagram-to-code parsing, code editing, and visual question answering across standard and agentic setups. An evaluation of 12 leading models reveals that while current MLLMs reason well on visual question answering, they struggle significantly with generating and editing code (such as LaTeX TikZ) from diagrams. Furthermore, the benchmark demonstrates that while agentic workflows generally improve code-generation tasks, they unexpectedly degrade question-answering accuracy for most models.

## [A Framework for Designing Reward Functions: From Objectives to Features to Human-Aligned Reward Functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12302v1)
**Score:** 0.869  |  **arXiv:** 2608.12302v1

This paper introduces a formal, three-step framework that enables non-experts to construct human-aligned linear reward functions directly from natural language task descriptions. The proposed pipeline combines a guided workflow to extract task features, a polynomial-time max-flow algorithm on causal graphs to select key reward terms, and a geometric preference-elicitation technique to fit feature weights. With theoretical guarantees including conflict-free weight spaces and $O(n \log \kappa)$ query complexity, this work is especially relevant for researchers interested in mathematically grounded reward engineering and efficient alignment from human feedback.
