## [PsychoAgent: An Affect-Sensitive Cognitive Architecture for Conflict-Aware Memory in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07438v1)
**Score:** 0.953  |  **arXiv:** 2608.07438v1
**Length:** 12 pages

**Summary for Researchers:**

PsychoAgent is a cognitive architecture for LLM agents that separates factual and emotional (affective) memories to better emulate human-like recall during complex or conflicting scenarios. By combining semantic filtering with emotional salience re-ranking, the system significantly outperforms standard RAG baselines at retrieving conflict-critical memories (0.933 vs. 0.667 recall) with only a minor trade-off in topical similarity. Human evaluations and qualitative traces demonstrate that this approach enables realistic cognitive behaviors like persistent affect, offline memory recombination, and selective reweighting.

## [TEPA: Revoking Stale Memories for Conflict-Robust Language Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07429v1)
**Score:** 0.939  |  **arXiv:** 2608.07429v1
**Length:** 14 pages

This paper introduces TEPA, a revocable memory framework for language agents designed to eliminate "memory pollution," where outdated facts persist and conflict with fresh evidence. By explicitly tracking validity, TEPA invalidates stale memories when contradictory information arrives under the same key while preserving the revoked history for auditing and potential re-promotion. Experiments demonstrate that TEPA vastly outperforms standard append-only and last-write-wins memory strategies during dynamic environment and preference shifts, making it a compelling read for researchers working on long-term agent state management.

## [CoinRAG: Contextualized Information Nugget KV Cache Reuse for Long-Context RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07458v1)
**Score:** 0.909  |  **arXiv:** 2608.07458v1

**CoinRAG** addresses the redundancy and prefill latency issues of full-chunk KV cache reuse in long-context Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) by caching fine-grained, query-relevant "nuggets" of information. It uses a two-stage retrieval process to identify key semantic units within chunks and seamlessly assembles their pre-computed KV cache slices alongside chunk-level context. Tested on multi-hop QA benchmarks, CoinRAG establishes a superior latency-accuracy trade-off, delivering an average 5.3% relative improvement in F1 score under tight prefill latency budgets.

## [Blast Radius](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07440v1)
**Score:** 0.885  |  **arXiv:** 2608.07440v1

To address token waste and affordability issues in agentic coding, the authors introduce Blast Radius, a predictive memory management layer that estimates prompt reach and reversibly archives unused context. Across evaluations on seven OpenAI models, the system reduced token consumption by 17–26%, achieved the lowest overflow rate among tested policies, and demonstrated byte-exact reversibility with zero improper recalls. The paper also establishes a formal foundation over a Polish context space that links context entropy to eviction and resurrection probabilities to make LLM context usage more efficient and sustainable.

This paper frames test-time scaling as a dynamic compute-allocation problem, balancing inference resources across answer generation, lightweight verification, and heavy evaluation. ## [Diffusion LLMs as Targets and Adversaries: Mechanistic Safety Exploits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07430v1)
**Score:** 0.855  |  **arXiv:** 2608.07430v1

This paper reveals that Diffusion Large Language Models (DLLMs) inherit sparse, transferable "safety neurons" from their autoregressive predecessors, making their safety alignment highly vulnerable to direct neuron pruning. Leveraging these internal mechanics, the authors introduce *SN-Guided Diffusion*, a fast, offline black-box jailbreak framework that steers the generation process away from safety triggers using a weighted safety neuron loss. The proposed attack achieves high transfer success rates across prominent open-source and proprietary targets (such as Llama-3, Qwen2.5, and Gemini-2.5) with orders-of-magnitude lower generation costs than prior frameworks.
