## [Pandora's AI Model Routing Box: Efficient Allocation with Costly Value Estimation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20316v1)
**Score:** 0.938  |  **arXiv:** 2608.20316v1

This paper tackles the trade-off in AI query routing where accurately predicting which specialist model will perform best is itself computationally expensive. By formalizing this as a classical "Pandora's Box" search problem with costly inspection, the authors derive closed-form policies for both centralized routing and decentralized bidding that decide when refining a performance estimate is worth the cost. Experiments across multi-LLM, RAG, and variable-reasoning benchmarks demonstrate that this approach matches the accuracy of exhaustive estimation while significantly reducing expensive evaluation calls.

## [SPADE: Self-Play in Adaptive Synthetic Executable Environments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19197v1)
**Score:** 0.919  |  **arXiv:** 2608.19197v1

**SPADE** is a self-play reinforcement learning framework where a single LLM acts both as an *Environment Designer*—writing executable, Gym-style multi-turn tasks—and a *Reasoning Agent* that learns to solve them. By optimizing a hint-based regret signal alongside document grounding and environment memory, the designer continually generates feasible challenges right at the boundary of the agent's capabilities. At a 30B-parameter scale, this adaptive curriculum significantly outperforms fixed-environment baselines across diverse math, coding, scientific reasoning, and multi-turn tool-use benchmarks.

## [Which Eviction Policy Should an LLM Cache Use? A Systematic Study Across Workloads, Capacities, and Encoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20280v1)
**Score:** 0.894  |  **arXiv:** 2608.20280v1
**Length:** 11 pages

This systematic evaluation reveals that no semantic cache eviction policy meaningfully outperforms standard Least Frequently Used (LFU)—improving on it by at most 0.041 percentage points—making LFU the strongest simple default. More critically, the authors discover a severe validity issue: typical similarity thresholds yield answer-substitutable responses only 2.1–3.9% of the time, causing raw hit rates of 51–60% to plunge to quality-adjusted rates of 1.1–2.2%. Ultimately, the paper demonstrates that ensuring answer validity and tuning non-transferable encoder thresholds are far more vital for LLM caching than fine-tuning eviction policies.

## [Beyond the Transcript: Detecting Covert Co ordination in Latent Multi-Agent Communication](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19161v1)
**Score:** 0.890  |  **arXiv:** 2608.19161v1

This paper introduces Verifiable Latent Alignments (VLA), a framework designed to detect and disrupt covert coordination between LLM agents communicating through hidden internal activations rather than visible text transcripts. By combining representation anomaly detection, counterfactual analysis, and steerability interventions, VLA can monitor private communication channels without needing to be trained on attack examples. Evaluated on multi-agent auction benchmarks, the approach achieves high detection accuracy (up to 0.993 AUROC) and significantly curbs collusive behavior across both homogeneous and heterogeneous agent systems.

## [Learning When to Think: Adaptive Reasoning for Test-Time Compute Allocation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20256v1)

The learned allocation also transfers zero-shot to other benchmarks, yielding up to a 76% token reduction on easier tasks like GSM8K.

## [ConceptGuard: Benchmarking Context-Sensitive Unlearning in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20338v1)
**Score:** 0.877  |  **arXiv:** 2608.20338v1
**Length:** 17 pages

The authors introduce **ConceptGuard**, a new benchmark designed to evaluate context-sensitive LLM unlearning for "dual-use" concepts—requiring models to eliminate harmful applications of an idea while preserving its benign, useful contexts. Unlike existing benchmarks that only test disjoint, fact-level forgetting, ConceptGuard evaluates whether methods can achieve true intent- and concept-level separation across complementary retain and forget sets. Experiments reveal that current unlearning techniques perform poorly under this setup, exposing weak contextual control and steep trade-offs between safety and utility.

## [Beyond Teacher Likelihood: Group-Calibrated On-Policy Distillation for Long-Context Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19181v1)
**Score:** 0.873  |  **arXiv:** 2608.19181v1
**Length:** 20 pages

In long-context reasoning, standard on-policy distillation (OPD) often favors locally plausible tokens that fail global task requirements evaluated by response-level verifiers. To fix this mismatch, the authors introduce Group-Calibrated OPD (GC-OPD), which calculates a group-normalized disagreement residual between teacher scores and verifier rewards, distributing this correction back across individual tokens. Evaluating across five long-context benchmarks, GC-OPD consistently outperforms vanilla OPD on 4B and 8B models by successfully harmonizing dense token-level distillation with global task outcomes.
