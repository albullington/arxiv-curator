**Score:** 0.973  |  **arXiv:** 2610.02202v1

## [Cogentic: Multi-Agent Orchestration for Automated Proof Discovery](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40324v1)
**Score:** 0.904  |  **arXiv:** 2609.40324v1

Powered by Gemini, the system discovered novel, expert-verified proofs for five previously open problems across online learning, auction theory, and mechanism design. It is a worthwhile read for researchers interested in multi-agent orchestration and pushing LLMs beyond single-shot generation to achieve genuine research-level reasoning.

## [Turbo Harness: Instance-Adaptive Harness Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40330v1)
**Score:** 0.894  |  **arXiv:** 2609.40330v1

Unlike traditional methods that apply a single, static harness across all tasks, **Turbo Harness** adapts agent harnesses to individual problem instances. It works by recycling artifacts from prior global optimization runs into a structured "playbook," which a trained editor model uses at test time to generate instance-specific harness patches. Evaluated across seven agent and software engineering benchmarks, this instance-adaptive approach consistently outperforms standard global harness baselines.

## [How Much of a Harness Does a Strong Agent Need for Autonomous ML Engineering?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40303v1)
**Score:** 0.894  |  **arXiv:** 2609.40303v1

This paper investigates whether the increasingly complex multi-agent scaffolding used in autonomous machine learning (MLE) systems is truly necessary. Through systematic benchmarks using identical frontier LLMs, the authors find that a simple agent with basic environment access (read, write, bash) matches the performance of elaborate, state-of-the-art harnesses under equal time budgets. Ultimately, the study concludes that benchmark success is driven primarily by the underlying model rather than hand-crafted frameworks, making complex agent scaffolding largely redundant.

## [KaliBench: A Fine-Grained Benchmark for Cybersecurity Tool Use on Kali Linux with Runtime-Free Verifiable Rewards](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02206v1)

## [Scaling Laws for Looped Mixture of Experts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40316v1)
**Score:** 0.877  |  **arXiv:** 2609.40316v1
**Length:** 19 pages

This paper introduces the first unified scaling law that jointly models recurrence (looped transformers) and sparsity (Mixture-of-Experts) alongside data and model size to guide compute- and memory-constrained architecture design. The framework accurately predicts held-out loss and reveals that looping and MoE sparsity offer complementary efficiency gains across active and total parameter budgets. Demonstrating this at a trillion-token scale, the authors show that an optimally looped MoE matches the reasoning performance of a non-looped MoE twice its size at the same training compute, while also enabling test-time compute scaling.

## [Reconstruct, Practice, Go Real: Guided Self-Improvement for Embodied Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02204v1)
**Score:** 0.867  |  **arXiv:** 2610.02204v1
**Length:** 17 pages

**RPG (Reconstruct, Practice, Go Real)** is a framework that autonomously improves robot manipulation capabilities without updating model weights by practicing in simulation to iteratively refine symbolic skills and multimodal LLM prompts. Across 22 simulated tasks, the approach dramatically increased success rates from 28.6% to 95.0%, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines. Crucially, the resulting frozen system transferred reliably to a physical robot, achieving a 100% success rate across 30 real-world trials.

## [From Gradients to Capabilities: Understanding Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02179v1)
**Score:** 0.865  |  **arXiv:** 2610.02179v1

This paper investigates the underlying optimization mechanics of multi-teacher on-policy distillation, analyzing how disparate teacher signals translate into student parameter updates and task performance. The authors reveal that Adam’s momentum significantly homogenizes diverse teacher gradients, BF16 rounding masks nearly 90% of subtle master-weight updates, and the choice between token- and response-level loss averaging heavily skews response weighting. Crucially, they demonstrate that while top-64 vocabulary KL approximation reliably mimics full-gradient updates, downstream capabilities like math accuracy hinge critically on these underlying loss-averaging strategies.

## [Semifactual Credit-Augmented Policy Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40360v1)
**Score:** 0.864  |  **arXiv:** 2609.40360v1

Current reinforcement learning methods for LLM reasoning (like GRPO) assign uniform credit to every generated token, which can inadvertently reinforce brittle, spurious dependencies on irrelevant prompt phrasing. To fix this, the authors introduce SCAPO, an algorithm that tests responses against semifactual prompt perturbations and reduces training rewards for unstable, highly sensitive tokens. When evaluated on Qwen models, SCAPO consistently outperforms GRPO on challenging math tasks—such as improving AIME accuracy by up to 5.6 percentage points—and across all tested out-of-distribution reasoning benchmarks.
