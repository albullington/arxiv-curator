## [Decomposition Buys Integrity, Not Yield](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17464v1)
**Score:** 0.920  |  **arXiv:** 2609.17464v1

Through theoretical modeling and production trace analysis, this paper demonstrates that hierarchical multi-agent architectures drastically reduce the amount of information that reaches the root agent, making flat architectures optimal for maximizing discovery ("yield"). Instead of improving findings, adding agent tiers acts as a trade-off that protects the root context from overflow and lowers compute costs at scale, but incurs heavy penalties in lost findings and alignment drift. Ultimately, the authors show that delegating tasks across agent hierarchies is mathematically justified in only 0.7% to 11.3% of real-world sessions, challenging core architectural folklore in multi-agent design.

## [What Breaks Under Pruning in Smart Homes, and When? Evaluating LLM Degradation Across Architectures and Task Complexity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17515v1)
**Score:** 0.889  |  **arXiv:** 2609.17515v1

This paper systematically evaluates how model pruning impacts smart-home tool calling across dense and mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures. The authors find that MoE models tolerate pruning significantly better than dense models, which degrade sharply and tend to develop systematic over-refusal. Crucially, pruning breaks fine-grained execution details (like specific arguments and values) long before high-level intent, demonstrating why evaluating pruned tool-calling agents requires looking beyond aggregate accuracy.

## [When Should LLMs Abstain? Chain-of-Self-Questioning for Selective Risk Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17516v1)
**Score:** 0.871  |  **arXiv:** 2609.17516v1

This paper introduces Chain-of-Self-Questioning (CoSQ), a prompt-only framework that enables large language models to evaluate their own factual support to decide whether to answer a query or abstain. Evaluated across 11 model families on TruthfulQA and Natural Questions, CoSQ reduced incorrect answer commitments by over 30% compared to standard chain-of-thought prompting while maintaining high coverage and improving accuracy on answered items. It offers a practical, easily tunable method for selective risk control in high-stakes applications where wrong answers are more damaging than admitting uncertainty.

## [Coupled Calibration and Learning: Mitigating Teacher Bias in LLM Distillation without Target-Domain Reward Feedback](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17474v1)
**Score:** 0.845  |  **arXiv:** 2609.17474v1

This paper introduces Coupled Calibration and Learning (CCL), an LLM distillation framework that prevents smaller student models from inheriting systematic teacher biases under covariate shift without requiring reward feedback in the target domain. The algorithm alternates between calibrating the teacher using only source-domain feedback and updating the student on target tasks, creating a mutually reinforcing loop between the two models. Theoretically, the authors prove that CCL converges to the optimal target student policy at a polynomial rate, whereas standard direct imitation can fail to eliminate teacher bias.
