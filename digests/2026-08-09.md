## [TRAJDEBUG: Tracing Error Lifecycle to Identify Critical Failures in Long-Horizon Agent Trajectories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06346v1)
**Score:** 0.925  |  **arXiv:** 2608.06346v1

To address the challenge of pinpointing root-cause errors in long LLM agent trajectories, the authors introduce **TrajDebug**, a framework that traces error lifecycles using history compression and evidence-based analysis to locate and attribute critical failures. They also present **TrajErrBench**, a benchmark of 486 manually annotated failed trajectories across realistic coding and tool-use scenarios. Evaluated across diverse benchmarks, TrajDebug outperforms existing baselines and generates actionable diagnoses that improve downstream agent success rates.

## [Beyond Top-K: Replacing Black-Box Retrieval with Interpretable Agentic Operations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06305v1)
**Score:** 0.915  |  **arXiv:** 2608.06305v1

Standard embedding-based retrieval often fails on complex financial and regulatory documents because chunking severs crucial relationships between numbers, headers, and units. To solve this, the authors introduce READ, an interpretable, embedding-free agent that navigates raw documents using deterministic lexical search and structural operations to create a replayable audit trail. Evaluated on a 780-page government financial report, READ significantly outperforms dense retrieval (58.8% accuracy vs. 15.7%), demonstrating that structured, lexical navigation is vastly superior to vector similarity for tabular and context-dependent text.

**Why this matches:** This paper matches your interest in novel agentic architectures by proposing a new approach that replaces standard black-box Top-K retrieval with interpretable agentic operations. ## [RRC: Unlocking Generative Reward Models in LLM Reinforcement Learning via Ranking-Based Reward Construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06310v1)
**Score:** 0.903  |  **arXiv:** 2608.06310v1

Generative reward models excel at relative response ranking, but struggle in LLM reinforcement learning because standard RL algorithms require absolute scalar scores. To bridge this gap, the authors propose Ranking-based Reward Construction (RRC), a method that converts comparative preference rankings into effective scalar RL signals using self-competitive sampling and anchor-guided reference responses. Experiments across open-ended chat and reasoning benchmarks show that RRC significantly improves RL training efficiency and performance compared to existing reward conversion approaches.

## [AV-AIVAT: 74x Cheaper Agent Evaluation with Certified Anytime-Valid Stopping in Imperfect-Information Games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06362v1)
**Score:** 0.896  |  **arXiv:** 2608.06362v1
**Length:** 34 pages

Evaluating game-playing agents is expensive because proving a true skill difference through raw outcomes requires thousands of hands to filter out luck. To solve this, the authors introduce **AV-AIVAT**, which combines variance-reduction techniques with continuously monitored confidence sequences to enable safe, statistically valid early stopping the moment a result is conclusive. Tested on poker benchmarks with LLM agents, the method reduces the required number of game hands by up to 74x while providing exact, third-party auditable statistical guarantees.

**Why this matches:** This paper aligns with your interest in LLM evaluation and AI builder tooling, sharing the overlapping keywords "agents" and "evaluation." It presents a practical approach to agent evaluation by introducing a method that makes evaluating agents in imperfect-information games 74x cheaper.

## [The Bitter Lesson of Tool Calling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06370v1)
**Score:** 0.891  |  **arXiv:** 2608.06370v1

This paper systematically compares programmatic tool calling—where LLMs execute tools via Python scripts—against traditional JSON function calling across 14 language models on the BFCL v4 benchmark. The authors find that programmatic tool calling matches or outperforms native JSON in 11 of 14 models, demonstrating particular strength in parallel task execution and superior resilience to long-context degradation. Overall, the study shows that code-driven tool execution is a robust, scalable alternative to rigid JSON calls that naturally improves alongside general model capabilities.

## [Learning When to Trust via Selective Context Preference Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06377v1)
**Score:** 0.871  |  **arXiv:** 2608.06377v1

To prevent language models from either blindly trusting misleading context or ignoring useful information entirely, this paper frames context reliance as a "selective trust" problem and introduces the **MIST** benchmark alongside a new vulnerability metric (**SC2W**). The authors propose **SCOPE**, a preference optimization method that trains models on balanced preference pairs across clean, misleading, correct, and irrelevant contexts using Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). Their approach significantly reduces a model's susceptibility to misleading signals while preserving its performance when given accurate or neutral external context.

## [The Low Frequency Trap: Video Language Models Fail at Simple Event Bookkeeping](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06361v1)
**Score:** 0.863  |  **arXiv:** 2608.06361v1

To isolate why video language models (VLMs) fail at temporal reasoning, this paper introduces a controlled, trace-grounded benchmark that evaluates event counting at the timestamp level across varying event frequencies and counts. The authors reveal that top models struggle dramatically with transient and high-frequency events, and that simply increasing frame sampling rates inflates final count scores without actually recovering the correct event sequence. This work is an important read for researchers focusing on fine-grained video understanding, temporal evaluation benchmarks, or the hidden failure modes of current video LLMs.

## [CalibForge: Adversarial Solver Calibration for Scaling Learnable Terminal Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06352v1)
**Score:** 0.862  |  **arXiv:** 2608.06352v1

This paper introduces **CalibForge**, an autonomous system that generates effective training data for terminal agents by using adversarial solver feedback to calibrate task difficulty into an optimal "learnable zone." By iteratively refining tasks based on how different solvers succeed or fail, the authors created a dataset of over 5,400 calibrated tasks that offer better supervision than standard task-authoring approaches. Models trained on this data achieved dramatic performance gains across major benchmarks, including up to 27–30 percentage point improvements on SWE-bench Pro and Doc2Repo.
