## [RATIO: A Benchmark for Retrieval Across Typed Ideation Operations in Scientific Literature](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27394v1)
**Score:** 0.926  |  **arXiv:** 2608.27394v1

This paper introduces **RATIO**, a large-scale benchmark designed to evaluate how effectively retrieval models find scientific papers to support three distinct ideation goals: directly solving a problem (*Address*), generalizing it (*Broaden*), or finding concrete implementations (*Specify*). Constructed from millions of computer science papers using distant supervision alongside LLM and human vetting, RATIO provides both training and evaluation frameworks for literature-grounded discovery systems. The authors show that while fine-tuning models on these specific ideation operations boosts retrieval performance, current systems still leave substantial room for improvement.

## [TraceML: An Empirical Analysis of Human-Agent Planning in Machine Learning Development](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26086v1)
**Score:** 0.902  |  **arXiv:** 2608.26086v1

## [Prefix Sliding for efficient test-time scaling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26070v1)
**Score:** 0.888  |  **arXiv:** 2608.26070v1
**Length:** 28 pages

The authors introduce Prefix Sliding, a method that mitigates the memory bottleneck of long reasoning chains by retaining only the initial prompt (prefix) and a sliding window of the most recent tokens. Without retraining, this approach speeds up existing language models by 3x with no loss in accuracy, outperforming standard sliding-window and summarization baselines. Additionally, training with Prefix Sliding via reinforcement learning enables models to scale efficiently to reasoning traces beyond 100,000 tokens.
