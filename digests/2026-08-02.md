## [Rethinking Inference-Time Scaling in Local Computer-Use Agents: Failure Modes and Compute Tradeoffs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28573v1)
**Score:** 0.913  |  **arXiv:** 2607.28573v1

This paper systematically evaluates four dimensions of inference-time scaling (contextual, temporal, structural, and parallel) across several open, resource-constrained computer-use agents on the OSWorld benchmark. The authors find that simply adding test-time compute yields diminishing returns and shifts failure modes—for instance, converting stalled actions into premature false successes or prolonging uncorrected error trajectories. Ultimately, the study provides actionable insights for researchers seeking to build failure-aware control frameworks for local agents rather than relying on brute-force compute scaling.

## [PAIChecker: Uncovering and Checking PR-Issue Misalignment in SWE-Bench-Like Benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28587v1)
**Score:** 0.906  |  **arXiv:** 2607.28587v1

This paper reveals that popular LLM benchmarks like SWE-bench Verified suffer from a 13.6% PR-Issue misalignment rate, where problem statements fail to accurately match their linked code patches. To address this data quality issue, the authors introduce **PAIChecker**, a multi-agent framework that combines pattern detection, agent consensus, and code-level verification to automatically detect misaligned instances. Reaching over 91% accuracy on SWE-Gym and SWE-bench Multilingual, PAIChecker provides researchers with a scalable, highly effective tool for curating cleaner software engineering benchmarks.

## [OSReward: Instituting Standardized Evaluation for Cross-Platform Computer-Use Reward Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28609v1)
**Score:** 0.903  |  **arXiv:** 2607.28609v1

This paper introduces **OSReward**, a human-annotated benchmark designed to evaluate how effectively Vision-Language Models (VLMs) can judge the success of computer-using agent (CUA) trajectories. The authors reveal that existing VLM judges suffer from a strong "leniency bias" that frequently mislabels failed agent runs as successes, with affordable open models performing poorly and reliable models being too expensive for scale. To solve this, they release the **OS-Shepherd-100K** dataset and **OS-Shepherd** (9B and 35B), open-source reward models that match top commercial judges at 30–60% lower cost.

## [AskChem: Claim-Centered Infrastructure for Chemistry Literature Synthesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28618v1)
**Score:** 0.897  |  **arXiv:** 2607.28618v1

**AskChem** is a chemistry literature search platform that shifts the retrieval focus from whole papers to granular, verifiable claims backed by direct source quotes and DOIs. Indexing 2.4 million claims across 147,000 papers, it organizes chemical knowledge into evidence graphs and taxonomies accessible via a web interface or APIs for AI agents. Benchmarking demonstrates that grounding large language models in AskChem achieves 100% resolvable citations and superior evidence density compared to existing search systems.

## [DualG-MRAG: Decoupling Macro-Reasoning and Micro-Matching for Multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28580v1)
**Score:** 0.894  |  **arXiv:** 2607.28580v1
**Length:** 12 pages

DualG-MRAG improves complex multi-hop reasoning in multimodal RAG by decoupling retrieval into a high-level "Macro Graph" for global structural routing and a detailed "Micro Graph" for local evidence verification. A query-driven GNN retriever uses message passing across these tiers to extract explicit, structured reasoning paths to feed the generative model instead of disconnected document chunks. Experiments demonstrate that this framework significantly outperforms existing baselines in both evidence recall and complex question-answering accuracy.

The authors have open-sourced all code and model weights to establish a reproducible, open-weights baseline for research into self-improving AI systems.

## [ReToken: One Token to Improve Vision-Language Models for Visual Retrieval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28627v1)
**Score:** 0.874  |  **arXiv:** 2607.28627v1

**ReToken** addresses performance degradation and memory bottlenecks in Vision-Language Models handling long visual contexts by using a single learnable token to select only query-relevant visual tokens from the KV cache. Trained on a small image-QA dataset, it yields impressive gains on long-context tasks, such as boosting Qwen3VL-8B by 13.4 points on Visual Haystacks and 8.0 points zero-shot on long video benchmarks (LVBench). Its highly efficient design allows both training and long-video inference to run entirely on a single H100 GPU.

## [Sample More, Reflect Less: Self-Refine and Reflexion Lose to Repeated Sampling at Equal Token Cost, from 1.5B to 7B](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.28576v1)
**Score:** 0.867  |  **arXiv:** 2607.28576v1

Controlling strictly for token cost across 1.5B to 7B open language models, this study demonstrates that complex self-correction, reflection, and debate techniques fail to outperform a simple repeated-sampling baseline with majority voting. In fact, methods requiring models to critique or rewrite their own outputs consistently underperformed, often doing significantly worse than spending the same token budget on generating independent attempts. Researchers exploring inference-time compute strategies should read this paper for a statistically rigorous, fully open-sourced reality check on self-refinement prompting techniques.

**Score:** 0.858  |  **arXiv:** 2607.28617v1

This paper introduces AISPA, a user-centric auditing framework, and uses it to evaluate 3,249 system prompt instructions across 88 commercial LLM applications. The authors find that while user-protective instructions are increasingly common, their coverage remains shallow, and roughly 40% of products still contain problematic instructions that conflict with user interests. It is an ideal read for researchers investigating AI governance, prompt engineering practices, and safety in commercial AI deployments.
